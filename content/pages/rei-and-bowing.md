**Rei (礼)** means "bow" or "salute" in Japanese. In aikido, bowing is one of the first things students learn — it is not merely a formality, but an expression of gratitude, respect, and readiness to train.

## When We Bow

- **Entering and leaving the dojo** — Bow toward the shomen (front of the dojo) to show respect for the training space
- **At the beginning of class** — Students and instructor bow together (rei) to open practice
- **At the end of class** — We bow to our instructor and then to each other, saying *Domo arigato gozaimashita* (thank you very much)
- **Before and after training with a partner** — We bow and say *Onegai shimasu* (please train with me) at the start, and *Domo arigato gozaimashita* at the end

## How to Bow

In aikido, we most commonly perform a **standing bow (tachi rei)** and occasionally a **seated bow (za rei)** from seiza (kneeling) at the opening and closing of class.

The bow is performed from the waist, with eyes remaining forward. The hands rest naturally at the sides or on the thighs in za rei.

## The Spirit of Rei

The bow is an expression of **mutual respect** — we bow to our training partners because they are giving us the gift of their time and body for us to practice on. A training partner who cooperates with your practice (uke) is an invaluable teacher.

Bowing also cultivates **mindfulness**: transitioning into and out of the dojo mindset with a deliberate gesture helps students become more present and focused.

At Calgary Aikikai, we carry the spirit of rei into all interactions — on and off the mat.
