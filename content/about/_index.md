## History

Calgary Aikikai was founded in 1980 by **Yasuhisa Inaba Shihan**. He arrived in Calgary from Japan that year to work for Petro-Canada in the oil and gas industry, bringing his aikido practice with him. Classes began with just three children students in the basement of a private residence.

At the time, Inaba Shihan held Ni-Dan (2nd degree black belt), having trained under **Yasuo Kobayashi Shihan** and **Kazuo Igarashi Shihan** at the Motosumiyoshi Aikido dojo in Japan.

### Dojo Timeline

- **1980** — Classes begin at Tano residence basement; first demonstration at CFB Calgary
- **1985** — Moved to Dr. Carl Safran Centre through a partnership with the Calgary Board of Education
- **1995** — Relocated to commercial space in Manchester industrial area; Inaba Shihan resigned from Petro-Canada to devote himself to aikido and his Japanese acupuncture/Shiatsu medicine practice
- **2008** — Relocated to River Park Church (current location)
- **2008** — Inaba Shihan suffered a stroke in August
- **2009** — Yasuhisa Inaba Shihan passed away on January 26, 2009, at age 56

### Legacy

Under Inaba Shihan's teaching, the dojo introduced hundreds of students to aikido. His philosophy centred on "harmony and developing wonderful Ki." Since his passing, the dojo continues as a collective under the guidance of his senior students. Several of his students have gone on to establish their own dojos.

Today, Calgary Aikikai is led by **Arran Fisher Sensei (5th Dan)** as Dojo-cho, with technical direction from **Kazuo Igarashi Shihan (8th Dan)**, who regularly visits Calgary from Japan.

## Inaba Shihan

**Yasuhisa Inaba Shihan** (November 26, 1952 – January 26, 2009), 6th Dan

Inaba Shihan was born in Osaka, Japan. After overcoming childhood heart surgery, he trained in Jujutsu, Judo, and Shorinji Kempo before discovering aikido. He began aikido training at age 21 under **Yasuo Kobayashi Shihan (8th Dan)** in Tokyo, and also studied **Tenshin Shoden Katori Shinto-ryu**, a traditional martial system founded circa 1387.

Alongside the dojo, he operated the Japan Shiatsu Clinic and taught Oriental Medicine through continuing education programs in Calgary, Edmonton, Germany, the USA, Mexico, and Japan.
